# Module 4: The Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO)

<Bridge>

This module gets around the dead end — and the way around it turns out to be the VAE's entire loss function.

## Maximising something you cannot compute

  prompt="You want to maximise a quantity you have no way of evaluating. Which of these is a workable strategy?"
  options={[
    "Give up on that quantity and optimise something unrelated instead",
    "Find a second quantity you CAN compute that is guaranteed never to exceed the first, and push that one up as high as possible"
  ]}

>

If a quantity you can compute always sits at or below the one you actually care about, then raising the computable one drags the target up with it — you may not know exactly where the target is, but you know it is at least as high as where you have pushed the bound.

That computable under-estimate is called a **lower bound**, and the specific one used here is the **Evidence Lower Bound**, or **ELBO** ("evidence" being the name for $\log p_\theta(x)$):

$$\log p_\theta(x) \ge \text{ELBO}(\theta, \phi; x)$$

The entire training procedure is: compute the ELBO, and maximise it.

</PredictReveal>

## What the ELBO actually says

Written out, the bound has two terms, and both are things a neural network can compute:

$$\text{ELBO} = \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z|x)}\big[\log p_\theta(x|z)\big]}_{\text{reconstruction}} - \underbrace{D_{KL}\big(q_\phi(z|x) \,\|\, p(z)\big)}_{\text{regulariser}}$$

In plain English: **"how well does the decoder rebuild $x$ from codes the encoder produced, minus how far the encoder's cloud has drifted from the standard normal prior."**

Training minimises the *negative* ELBO, which is the loss you will actually write in code:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{VAE}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}(x, \hat{x}) + D_{KL}\big(q_\phi(z|x) \,\|\, p(z)\big)$$

These are exactly the two forces Module 2 predicted — a reconstruction term and a term shaping the latent space — now derived rather than asserted.

| Term | Pulls toward | Left alone, it would |
|---|---|---|
| **Reconstruction** | Codes that rebuild $x$ accurately | Spread codes far apart to keep inputs distinguishable — the Module 2 failure |
| **KL to prior** | Codes clustered in the standard normal region | Collapse every input to the same cloud, discarding the input entirely |

>

A large KL means the encoder's clouds never came near the prior — so the latent space is once again irregular and full of gaps. Reconstruction of *training* inputs is fine, because those codes are exactly where the decoder was trained. But drawing $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ lands somewhere the encoder never used, and the decoder returns noise.

This is Module 2's failure mode re-appearing, and it shows the two terms are genuinely in tension: each one, unopposed, breaks the model in a different direction.

</PredictReveal>

## Why maximising the bound also improves the encoder

<PredictReveal
  prompt="Maximise the ELBO with respect to the encoder parameters ϕ, holding the decoder fixed. What happens to the gap between the ELBO and the true log p(x)?"
  options={[
    "The gap grows, because the encoder gains capacity",
    "The gap shrinks — and that gap is exactly the KL divergence to the true posterior we could not compute",
    "The gap is unaffected by ϕ"
  ]}
  correct={1}
>

The exact relationship, derived in the optional section below, is:

$$\log p_\theta(x) = \text{ELBO}(\theta, \phi; x) + D_{KL}\big(q_\phi(z|x) \,\|\, p_\theta(z|x)\big)$$

The left side does not depend on $\phi$ at all. So if raising the ELBO cannot move $\log p_\theta(x)$, and the two right-hand terms must still sum to it, then raising the ELBO must *lower* the other term by the same amount:

$$\arg\max_\phi \text{ELBO} \equiv \arg\min_\phi D_{KL}\big(q_\phi(z|x) \,\|\, p_\theta(z|x)\big)$$

This is the payoff for Module 3's dead end. We wanted the encoder to approximate the intractable true posterior, but could not compute that posterior to compare against. Maximising the ELBO does it anyway — the approximation improves as a side effect, without the intractable quantity ever being evaluated.

</PredictReveal>

<Recall items={[
  { q: "Why does maximising the ELBO improve the encoder's approximation of a posterior we cannot even compute?", a: "Because log p(x) = ELBO + KL(q(z|x) || p(z|x)), and log p(x) does not depend on the encoder parameters. The two right-hand terms must sum to a value the encoder cannot change, so any increase in the ELBO forces an equal decrease in the KL to the true posterior — improving the approximation without ever evaluating the intractable posterior." },

## Optional depth — the derivation in full

Everything above is enough to use, implement and reason about the ELBO. This section shows where the identity comes from. It is worth working through eventually, but nothing later in the track depends on having done so.

Start from the KL divergence between the encoder and the true posterior — the thing we wish we could minimise directly:

<DerivationSteps>
  <DerivationStep step={1} title="Definition of KL divergence">
    $$D_{KL}\big(q_\phi(z|x) \,\|\, p_\theta(z|x)\big) = \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z|x)}\left[ \log \frac{q_\phi(z|x)}{p_\theta(z|x)} \right]$$
  </DerivationStep>

  <DerivationStep step={2} title="Apply Bayes' rule to the true posterior">
    Substituting $p_\theta(z|x) = \frac{p_\theta(x, z)}{p_\theta(x)}$ turns the unknown posterior into the joint distribution and the evidence:
    $$= \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z|x)}\left[ \log \frac{q_\phi(z|x) \cdot p_\theta(x)}{p_\theta(x, z)} \right]$$
  </DerivationStep>

  <DerivationStep step={3} title="Split the logarithm">
    A log of a product becomes a sum of logs:
    $$= \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z|x)}\left[ \log q_\phi(z|x) - \log p_\theta(x, z) + \log p_\theta(x) \right]$$
  </DerivationStep>

  <DerivationStep step={4} title="Pull out the evidence">
    $\log p_\theta(x)$ contains no $z$, so its expectation over $z$ is just itself and it leaves the bracket untouched:
    $$= \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z|x)}\left[ \log q_\phi(z|x) - \log p_\theta(x, z) \right] + \log p_\theta(x)$$
  </DerivationStep>

  <DerivationStep step={5} title="Rearrange">
    Moving terms across gives the identity used above, with the bracketed expectation now recognisable as the ELBO:
    $$\log p_\theta(x) = \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z|x)}\left[ \log p_\theta(x, z) - \log q_\phi(z|x) \right]}_{\text{ELBO}} + D_{KL}\big(q_\phi(z|x) \,\|\, p_\theta(z|x)\big)$$
  </DerivationStep>
</DerivationSteps>

Since KL divergence is never negative (Module 1), the second term can only add — so the ELBO can never exceed $\log p_\theta(x)$. That is precisely what makes it a *lower* bound, with equality only when $q_\phi(z|x)$ matches the true posterior exactly.

Substituting $p_\theta(x,z) = p_\theta(x|z)p(z)$ into the ELBO and splitting the log recovers the two-term reconstruction-plus-KL form used throughout the rest of the track.

---

<ConceptBox title="TL;DR">
- $\log p_\theta(x)$ cannot be computed, so training maximises a computable **lower bound** on it instead — the **ELBO**. Pushing the bound up drags the target up with it.
- The ELBO splits into exactly two terms: a **reconstruction** term (rebuild $x$ from the sampled code) minus a **KL regulariser** (keep the encoder's cloud near the prior). The training loss is the negative of this.
- The two terms are in genuine tension — reconstruction alone recreates the gap-filled latent space of Module 2; KL alone collapses every input to the same cloud.
- Because $\log p_\theta(x) = \text{ELBO} + D_{KL}(q_\phi \| p_\theta(z|x))$ and the left side does not depend on $\phi$, maximising the ELBO **automatically** shrinks the gap to the intractable true posterior.
</ConceptBox>
