# Module 10: β-VAE & Disentangled Representations

<ConceptBox title="The Disentanglement Objective">
In a truly **disentangled representation**, each single latent dimension $z_j$ controls exactly one independent, human-interpretable generative factor (e.g. azimuth rotation, stroke thickness, lighting angle, scale) while remaining completely invariant to all other factors. **$\beta$-VAE** achieves this by placing an adjustable information bottleneck on the latent channel.
</ConceptBox>

<Bridge>
Module 9 showed that latent dimensions can represent high-level concepts. However, in standard VAEs ($\beta=1$), factors often remain entangled across multiple correlated dimensions. This module introduces $\beta$-VAE and analyzes the trade-off between disentanglement and reconstruction fidelity.
</Bridge>

## The $\beta$-VAE Formulation

Introduced by Higgins et al. (ICLR 2017), $\beta$-VAE modifies the standard ELBO objective by introducing a hyperparameter $\beta > 0$ weighting the KL divergence term:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\beta\text{-VAE}}(\theta, \phi; x) = \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z|x)}\big[\log p_\theta(x|z)\big] - \beta \, D_{KL}\big(q_\phi(z|x) \,\|\, p(z)\big)$$

- When $\beta = 1$: Standard VAE (exact ELBO).
- When $\beta > 1$: Stronger information bottleneck, encouraging statistical independence and axis alignment.
- When $\beta < 1$: Weaker regularization, prioritizing sharp reconstructions at the expense of latent structure.

## Why Does $\beta > 1$ Force Disentanglement?

The standard prior $p(z) = \mathcal{N}(0, I) = \prod_{j=1}^d \mathcal{N}(z_j; 0, 1)$ is **factorized** (all dimensions are statistically independent).

Under the **Information Bottleneck Principle**:
1. When $\beta > 1$, the encoder is severely restricted in the total number of nats/bits it can transmit through the latent channel $z$.
2. To reconstruct $x$ with minimal channel capacity, the network must discover the most efficient representation possible: the true, orthogonal, independent generative factors of the data.
3. Because the prior has diagonal covariance, the model aligns these independent factors with single coordinate axes.

When $\beta$ is excessively large, minimizing the KL divergence dominates the entire optimization. The easiest way for gradient descent to reduce loss is to set $\mu = 0$ and $\sigma^2 = 1$ everywhere.

When $q_\phi(z|x) = \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ contains zero mutual information about $x$, the decoder receives no input signal and simply learns to output the unconditional mean image $\bar{x}$. This failure mode is known as **Posterior Collapse**.

</PredictReveal>

## Strategies to Prevent Posterior Collapse

1. **KL Annealing / Warm-up**: Start training with $\beta = 0$, gradually increasing $\beta \to 1$ or $\beta_{\text{target}}$ over the first $N$ epochs.
2. **Free Bits / Minimum KL Constraint**: Replace $D_{KL}$ with $\max(\tau, D_{KL})$, giving each dimension a budget of $\tau$ bits for free before applying regularization.
3. **Controlled Capacity Increase ($C$-VAE)**: Gradually increase a target capacity $C$: $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}} - \gamma |D_{KL} - C|$.

<ConceptBox title="TL;DR">
- **$eta$-VAE** simply weights the KL term: $\mathcal{L} = 	ext{reconstruction} - eta \, D_{KL}$. $eta = 1$ is the ordinary ELBO.
- $eta > 1$ tightens the information bottleneck, forcing the encoder to spend limited capacity on the few genuinely independent factors — and because the prior is diagonal, those factors line up with individual axes (**disentanglement**).
- The cost is reconstruction sharpness, so $eta$ is a **dial between disentanglement and fidelity**, not a free improvement.
- Push $eta$ too far and you get **posterior collapse**: the encoder outputs the prior for every input, carrying zero information, and the decoder falls back to the dataset average. **KL annealing** and **free bits** are the standard defences.
</ConceptBox>
