# Module 7: Reconstruction Loss & Likelihood Assumptions

<ConceptBox title="Likelihood Dictates Loss">
In the ELBO, the reconstruction term is $\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z|x)}[\log p_\theta(x|z)]$. The specific loss function you use (e.g. Binary Cross-Entropy or Mean Squared Error) is not an arbitrary design choice — it is directly determined by the **probabilistic distribution assumed for the decoder's output** $p_\theta(x|z)$.
</ConceptBox>

## Case 1: Bernoulli Likelihood → Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE)

When data $x \in [0, 1]^D$ consists of pixel intensities treated as independent probabilities (common for MNIST or black-and-white images), we model $p_\theta(x|z)$ as a **multivariate Bernoulli distribution**:

$$p_\theta(x|z) = \prod_{i=1}^D \hat{x}_i^{x_i} (1 - \hat{x}_i)^{1 - x_i}, \qquad \text{where } \hat{x} = \text{Sigmoid}(\text{Decoder}_\theta(z))$$

Taking the logarithm:

$$\log p_\theta(x|z) = \sum_{i=1}^D \left[ x_i \log \hat{x}_i + (1 - x_i) \log(1 - \hat{x}_i) \right] = -\text{BCE}(x, \hat{x})$$

Therefore, maximizing the expected log-Bernoulli likelihood is exactly equivalent to **minimizing the Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss** summed over all pixels:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}(x, \hat{x}) = \sum_{i=1}^D \text{BCE}(x_i, \hat{x}_i)$$

## Case 2: Gaussian Likelihood → Mean Squared Error (MSE)

When data $x \in \mathbb{R}^D$ is continuous (e.g. normalized real-world RGB photographs or audio), we model $p_\theta(x|z)$ as an **isotropic Gaussian** with fixed variance $\sigma_x^2$:

$$p_\theta(x|z) = \mathcal{N}\big(x; \hat{x}, \sigma_x^2 I\big) = \frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma_x^2)^{D/2}} \exp\left( -\frac{\|x - \hat{x}\|^2}{2\sigma_x^2} \right)$$

Taking the logarithm:

$$\log p_\theta(x|z) = -\frac{D}{2}\log(2\pi\sigma_x^2) - \frac{1}{2\sigma_x^2} \|x - \hat{x}\|^2$$

Dropping the constant term, maximizing this log-likelihood is equivalent to **minimizing Mean Squared Error (MSE)** scaled by the assumed observation variance:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}(x, \hat{x}) \propto \frac{1}{2\sigma_x^2} \sum_{i=1}^D (x_i - \hat{x}_i)^2$$

| Likelihood Assumption $p_\theta(x|z)$ | Target Data Range | Decoder Final Layer | Resulting Loss Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bernoulli** | $x_i \in [0, 1]$ (e.g. MNIST) | `nn.Sigmoid()` | `F.binary_cross_entropy(recon_x, x, reduction='sum')` |
| **Gaussian (Fixed $\sigma_x^2$)** | $x_i \in \mathbb{R}$ (e.g. RGB) | Linear / Tanh | `F.mse_loss(recon_x, x, reduction='sum')` |
| **Gaussian (Learned $\sigma_x^2$)** | $x_i \in \mathbb{R}$ | Two output heads: $\hat{\mu}, \hat{\sigma}^2$ | Heteroscedastic Gaussian NLL |

**The reduction mismatch is the most common bug in VAE implementations.**

Mathematically, the ELBO sums both $\log p_\theta(x|z)$ over all $D$ observable dimensions and $D_{KL}$ over all $d$ latent dimensions for each sample:

```python
# CORRECT: Consistent sum over dimensions, mean over batch
recon_loss = F.binary_cross_entropy(recon_x, x, reduction='sum') / batch_size
kl_loss = -0.5 * torch.sum(1 + logvar - mu.pow(2) - logvar.exp()) / batch_size
total_loss = recon_loss + kl_loss
```

If you accidentally use `reduction='mean'` for reconstruction loss, the reconstruction term is divided by 784 (for MNIST), making it 784× too small relative to the KL penalty!

<ConceptBox title="TL;DR">
- The reconstruction loss is **not a free choice** — it follows from what distribution you assume the decoder outputs.
- Assume **Bernoulli** (pixels in $[0,1]$, sigmoid output) and the log-likelihood *is* negative **BCE**. Assume **Gaussian** (continuous values, fixed variance) and it *is* negative **MSE**.
- MSE reconstructions look blurry for a principled reason: under uncertainty the L2-minimising prediction is the *average* of the plausible outputs, and averaging sharp alternatives gives a soft edge.
- The **reduction trap** is the most common VAE bug: sum reconstruction over pixels and KL over latent dimensions, then divide both by batch size. Using `reduction='mean'` on reconstruction shrinks it ~784× against the KL term and collapses the latents.
</ConceptBox>
