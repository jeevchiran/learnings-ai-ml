<ConceptBox title="TL;DR">
- A brand-new product has an empty column, so pure collaborative filtering scores it **exactly zero** — not badly, but not at all. It can never be shown, so it never warms up.
- **Content and image embeddings break the loop**: a product photo and title place a new item near its neighbours before anyone has clicked it.
- Fusion happens at one of three points: **early** (concatenate features), **late** (blend scores), or **hybrid** (content initialises the ID embedding, behaviour takes over).
- The blend weight should be a **function of how much interaction data the item has**, not a constant.
</ConceptBox>

## The cold-start loop

NovaCart lists the ANC Headphones. Zero views, zero carts, zero purchases.

Item-item CF computes similarity from the interaction column, which is all zeros, so every cosine numerator is 0. The item cannot enter any top-k. Not being shown means not being interacted with, which keeps the column empty.

**Nothing inside the interaction data can break this.** The escape has to come from outside it — and the product page is full of signal that has nothing to do with behaviour.

## What a product actually carries

| Modality | Source | What it captures |
|---|---|---|
| **Text** | Title, description, specs | Category, brand, function, materials |
| **Image** | Product photo | Colour, shape, style — the things buyers respond to and never write down |
| **Structured** | Price, brand, category, attributes | Cheap, exact, already in your catalog DB |
| **Behavioural** | The interaction matrix | Cross-category patterns nothing else can see |

Encode text with a sentence transformer, images with a vision encoder (CLIP gives you both in one shared space, which is why it became the default), and you have a vector for every product on the day it is listed.

## The three fusion points

**Early fusion — concatenate.** Feed content embeddings alongside ID embeddings into one ranker: `[user_emb ‖ item_id_emb ‖ text_emb ‖ image_emb ‖ price]`. The model learns the interaction between modalities. Most expressive, most data-hungry, and it degrades gracefully when `item_id_emb` is a fresh random vector because the other blocks still carry signal.

**Late fusion — blend scores.** Run CF and a content model separately, then combine:

$$\text{score}(u,i) = (1-w)\cdot\text{score}_{\text{CF}}(u,i) + w\cdot\text{score}_{\text{content}}(u,i)$$

This is the widget's slider. Simple, debuggable, each side improvable independently. It cannot learn *interactions* between modalities — but it lets you set $w$ per item, which is the point below.

**Hybrid — content initialises, behaviour takes over.** A new item's embedding starts as a projection of its content vector; as interactions arrive, the learned ID embedding is trained and gradually dominates. Best of both, most machinery.

<ConceptBox title="The blend weight is not a constant">
Slide the widget to pure content: the ranking becomes "more of the same aisle" — safe, obvious, and blind to the cross-category patterns only behaviour reveals. No product photo says *people who buy yoga mats also buy steel bottles*.

Slide to pure behaviour: every new arrival is invisible.

Neither endpoint is right, and neither is any fixed midpoint. Make $w$ a decreasing function of the item's interaction count:

$$w_i = \frac{m}{m + n_i}$$

with $n_i$ the item's interactions and $m$ a constant around 50. A brand-new item is fully content-driven ($w=1$); an item with 500 interactions is 9% content ($w \approx 0.09$). Each product migrates across the slider on its own schedule as it warms up. This is the same shrinkage formula as target encoding in module 10, doing the same job: trust the specific evidence in proportion to how much of it you have.
</ConceptBox>

## The four cold-starts

They are different problems and people conflate them constantly:

| Cold | Fix |
|---|---|
| **New item** | Content/image embeddings — this module |
| **New user** | Popularity, then session-based recs from their first click; item-item CF works from click #1 |
| **New user *and* item** | Popularity within the item's category, plus business rules |
| **Systemic** (brand-new store) | You have no interaction data at all: content similarity and hand-curated rules until traffic accumulates |

Note that new-*user* cold start is much easier than new-*item*, because item-item CF starts producing recommendations after a single interaction — which is exactly why it stays in production alongside fancier models.

<ConceptBox title="Common confusion — content similarity is not relevance">
cos(ANC Headphones, Earbuds) = 0.95 from title and photo alone. That says they are the **same kind of thing**. It does not say a shopper who bought earbuds wants headphones — they may well have just solved that problem.

Content models are strong at **substitutes** ("more like this") and weak at **complements** ("goes with this"). Complements are almost pure behaviour: the laptop-stand-and-USB-hub relationship is invisible in the photos and obvious in the co-purchase data. Use content to cold-start and to fill "similar items"; use behaviour for "frequently bought together". Serving the wrong one is a visibly bad page.
</ConceptBox>

## Practical notes

- **Precompute.** Encoding a photo takes ~50 ms — impossible in a request. Encode on catalog ingest, store the vector, refresh when the listing changes.
- **Reuse the retrieval index.** Content vectors go into the same ANN index as behavioural vectors (module 8), so cold items are retrievable through the existing path with no new serving component.
- **Watch for duplicates.** A strong image encoder will rank near-identical listings as maximally similar, producing a "similar items" row of the same product nine times. Deduplicate on the content vector itself before serving — cluster at cosine > 0.98 and keep one per cluster.
