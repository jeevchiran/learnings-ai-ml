<ConceptBox title="TL;DR">
- A random variable turns outcomes into numbers so you can do arithmetic on uncertainty.
- **PMF gives probability; PDF gives density.** A PDF value can exceed 1 — only the *area* under it is a probability.
- Four workhorses: Bernoulli (one binary trial), Binomial (count of successes), Poisson (count of rare events per window), Gaussian (continuous noise).
- Choosing a loss quietly chooses a distribution: MSE ⟺ "residuals are Gaussian."
</ConceptBox>

## Random Variables

A **random variable** $X$ maps outcomes to numbers: a coin flip → 0 or 1, a dice roll → 1 through 6, tomorrow's temperature → a real number. Once outcomes are numbers, you can do arithmetic and statistics on them — that's the whole point.

<ConceptBox title="Discrete vs. continuous">
- **Discrete** — countable outcomes, described by a **PMF** (probability mass function) $P(X=k)$, which must sum to 1.
- **Continuous** — a continuum of outcomes, described by a **PDF** (probability density function) $f(x)$, where $P(a\le X\le b) = \int_a^b f(x)\,dx$, and the PDF integrates to 1. A PDF value itself is *not* a probability (it can exceed 1) — only areas under it are.
</ConceptBox>

## Four Distributions That Keep Showing Up in ML

| Distribution | Models | Shows up as |
|---|---|---|
| **Bernoulli(p)** | one binary trial | the label in binary classification |
| **Binomial(n,p)** | count of successes in n Bernoulli trials | A/B test conversions |
| **Poisson(λ)** | count of events in a fixed window | rare-event / count regression |
| **Gaussian(μ,σ²)** | continuous, symmetric, bell-shaped | noise assumption behind MSE loss, weight initialization |

$$P(X{=}k) = \binom{n}{k}p^k(1-p)^{n-k} \quad\text{(Binomial)} \qquad\qquad f(x) = \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}} \quad\text{(Gaussian)}$$

<ConceptBox title="Common confusion — why does MSE loss assume a Gaussian?">
Minimizing squared error is equivalent to maximum likelihood estimation (Module 8) *under the assumption that residuals are Gaussian-distributed noise around the true value*. That's not a coincidence — it's baked into the choice of loss function, and it's why MSE is sensitive to outliers (a Gaussian assigns them very low but nonzero probability).
</ConceptBox>

## In Python (SciPy)

```python
from scipy import stats

# Binomial: P(exactly 7 heads in 10 flips of a fair coin)
p_7 = stats.binom.pmf(7, n=10, p=0.5)

# Gaussian: probability density at x=0 for standard normal
density = stats.norm.pdf(0, loc=0, scale=1)

# Sample 1000 draws from Poisson(lambda=4)
counts = stats.poisson.rvs(mu=4, size=1000)
```

## Practice

**Problem 1.** A fair coin is flipped 10 times. What distribution models the number of heads, and what are its parameters?

**Solution:** Binomial(n=10, p=0.5).

**Problem 2.** A call center averages 4 calls per minute. Which distribution models the number of calls in the next minute?

**Solution:** Poisson(λ=4) — counting rare, independent events in a fixed window is the Poisson's defining use case.
