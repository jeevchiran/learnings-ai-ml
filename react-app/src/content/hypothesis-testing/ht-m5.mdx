**Blood pressure treatment.** A cardiologist tests whether a 12-week lifestyle program lowers systolic BP, measuring each of 15 patients before and after. ## Paired vs. Independent

Module 4's two groups were different people with no correspondence. In a paired design the same subject (or a matched pair) appears in **both** conditions:

- **Before/After** — same subject measured before and after a treatment.
- **Matched pairs** — two subjects matched on key traits; one treated, one control.
- **Crossover** — each subject gets both treatments in randomized sequence.

**Why pairing is powerful.** Patient A might naturally sit at 155 mmHg, patient B at 135 — variability with nothing to do with the treatment. Analyzed as independent groups, this person-to-person noise inflates the SE and hides the effect. Instead compute the difference $d_i = x_{\text{before},i} - x_{\text{after},i}$: since both readings are the same person, A's natural tendency cancels in the subtraction. What remains is within-person change — exactly the target. The paired t-test is therefore just a **one-sample t-test on the differences** $d_1,\ldots,d_n$.

## The Test Statistic

<DerivationSteps>
<DerivationStep number={1} title="Difference per pair">
$d_i = x_{1i} - x_{2i}$ (before − after). Pick the sign convention to match the hypothesis.
</DerivationStep>
<DerivationStep number={2} title="Hypotheses">
$H_0: \mu_d = 0$ (no average change); $H_1$ two-tailed ($\neq$), right ($>$), or left ($<$).
</DerivationStep>
<DerivationStep number={3} title="Mean and SD of differences">
$$\bar{d} = \frac{1}{n}\sum d_i, \qquad s_d = \sqrt{\frac{\sum(d_i - \bar{d})^2}{n-1}}$$
$\bar{d}$ estimates $\mu_d$; $s_d$ measures how consistent the effect is across subjects.
</DerivationStep>
<DerivationStep number={4} title="Statistic and null distribution">
$$t = \frac{\bar{d}}{s_d/\sqrt{n}} \sim t(n-1) \quad \text{under } H_0$$
The one-sample t-statistic applied to the differences. $n-1$ df because one parameter ($\bar{d}$) is estimated before $s_d$.
</DerivationStep>
<DerivationStep number={5} title="Decide">
Reject if $|t| > t^*(\alpha/2, n-1)$ (two-tailed) or $t > t^*(\alpha, n-1)$ (one-tailed), equivalently p-value $< \alpha$.
</DerivationStep>
</DerivationSteps>

<ConceptBox title="Assumptions">
1. **Random pairs** — randomly sampled, or randomized assignment (crossover).
2. **Normality of differences** — the $d_i$ approximately normal. Matters for $n < 30$ (check a histogram/QQ-plot); for $n \geq 30$ the CLT makes $\bar{d}$ approximately normal regardless.
3. **Independence between pairs** — within-pair dependence is intentional; different pairs must not influence each other.
4. **No extreme outliers in $d_i$** — they distort $\bar{d}$ and $s_d$; if present, consider the Wilcoxon signed-rank test.
</ConceptBox>

## Paired vs. Independent — When

| Feature | Paired | Independent |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | Same subject twice / matched pairs | Two unrelated groups |
| n per group | Equal (n pairs) | Can differ |
| df | $n-1$ | Welch, near $n_1+n_2-2$ |
| Between-subject noise | Removed by differencing | Present in SE, cuts power |
| Use when | Before/after, matched, crossover | Separate, unrelated groups |

<ConceptBox title="The design determines the test">
If data were collected paired, use the paired test. Using an independent test on paired data discards structure that reduces variability and raises power — you don't get to pick the simpler test; the data structure mandates the paired analysis.
</ConceptBox>

## Worked Example: Blood Pressure

Eight patients, systolic BP before/after, $d_i = $ before − after (positive = improvement). Right-tailed: $H_0: \mu_d = 0$ vs $H_1: \mu_d > 0$, $\alpha = 0.05$.

| Patient | Before | After | $d_i$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 152 | 144 | 8 |
| 2 | 143 | 137 | 6 |
| 3 | 160 | 149 | 11 |
| 4 | 137 | 128 | 9 |
| 5 | 148 | 140 | 8 |
| 6 | 155 | 145 | 10 |
| 7 | 142 | 136 | 6 |
| 8 | 138 | 130 | 8 |

**Mean:** $\bar{d} = 66/8 = 8.25$ mmHg. **SD:** squared deviations from 8.25 sum to 21.5, so $s_d = \sqrt{21.5/7} \approx 1.753$. **SE:** $1.753/\sqrt{8} \approx 0.620$.

**Statistic:** $t = 8.25/0.620 \approx 13.31$, df $= 7$.

**Decision:** right-tailed $t^*(0.05, 7) = 1.895$; $13.31 \gg 1.895$ → **reject $H_0$** (p ≈ 0.0000012).

**Conclusion:** Extremely strong evidence the program lowers systolic BP. Mean reduction 8.25 mmHg, 95% CI $8.25 \pm 2.365 \times 0.620 = [6.78, 9.72]$ mmHg.

## Practice

<ConceptBox title="Workshop scores">
6 students, pre: 62, 71, 58, 75, 68, 64; post: 74, 82, 70, 84, 76, 78. $d_i = $ post − pre. $H_0: \mu_d = 0$ vs $H_1: \mu_d > 0$, $\alpha = 0.05$.

Differences: 12, 11, 12, 9, 8, 14. $\bar{d} = 66/6 = 11$. Squared deviations sum to 24, $s_d = \sqrt{24/5} \approx 2.191$. SE $= 2.191/\sqrt{6} \approx 0.895$.

$t = 11/0.895 \approx 12.29$, df $= 5$. Critical $t^*(0.05,5) = 2.015$; $12.29 \gg 2.015$ → **reject $H_0$**. Strong evidence the workshop improves scores, mean gain ≈ 11 points.
</ConceptBox>
