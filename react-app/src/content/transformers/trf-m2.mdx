# Module 2: Encoder-Decoder in a Transformer

<Bridge>
Module 1 established the two flavours of attention a transformer uses: self-attention (a sequence reading itself) and cross-attention (one sequence reading another). This module is where they get assembled into the two halves of the original transformer — an encoder stack and a decoder stack — and what's different about each.
</Bridge>

## Two stacks, two jobs

<PredictReveal
  prompt="The encoder reads the full source sentence before translation starts — it already has the whole sentence in hand. The decoder generates the output one word at a time, left to right. Should the encoder's self-attention be allowed to look at future words in its own input?"
  options={["No — same restriction as the decoder", "Yes — the encoder already has the whole sentence, there's nothing to leak"]}
  correct={1}
>

The **encoder** stack runs self-attention with no mask at all: every word can attend to every other word, before and after it, because the encoder's whole job is to build the richest possible representation of an input it already has completely.

The **decoder** stack runs *masked* self-attention — the causal mask from Module 1's intuition, made concrete: when generating word 5, the decoder can only attend to words 1 through 5 of what it has generated so far, never word 6 onward, because word 6 doesn't exist yet. Predicting it by looking at it would be cheating during training and impossible at inference.

</PredictReveal>

## Where cross-attention comes back in

<PredictReveal
  prompt="A decoder block has masked self-attention over its own (partial) output. Where does information from the SOURCE sentence — the thing actually being translated — enter the decoder?"
  options={["It doesn't — the decoder only ever sees its own previous output", "Through a separate cross-attention layer, querying the encoder's output"]}
  correct={1}
>

Each decoder block has *two* attention layers, not one: masked self-attention over what it has generated so far, followed by cross-attention where the decoder's queries meet the *encoder's* keys and values. This is exactly the attention mechanism from the previous track's Modules 6–10 — a decoder step attending to encoder states — just now sitting inside a much larger, fully-attention-based architecture instead of wrapping an RNN.

$$\text{Attention}(Q_{\text{decoder}}, K_{\text{encoder}}, V_{\text{encoder}})$$

</PredictReveal>

---

<ConceptBox title="TL;DR">
- The transformer has two stacks: an **encoder** (unmasked self-attention, sees the whole input at once) and a **decoder** (masked self-attention, generates left to right).
- The encoder needs no causal mask — it already holds the complete input, so nothing is hidden from a future it doesn't have.
- The decoder's masked self-attention prevents attending to not-yet-generated positions — enforcing at training time the same left-to-right constraint that holds at inference time.
- A decoder block runs masked self-attention over its own output first, then **cross-attention** into the encoder's output — the exact mechanism the previous track covered, now embedded inside a fully-attention architecture.
</ConceptBox>
