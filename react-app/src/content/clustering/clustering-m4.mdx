<Bridge>
**The key insight.** A good clustering places each point closer to its own cluster than to any other — by a wide margin. The silhouette score quantifies that margin for every point, then averages them. WCSS can't: two clusters can each be tight yet sit right next to each other, overlapping at the boundary.
</Bridge>

## Why WCSS Isn't Enough

WCSS measures **cohesion** (tightness) but is blind to **separation**. Two failure modes with similar WCSS but poor clusterings:

- **Tight but too close** — two low-scatter clusters that nearly touch; boundary points could belong to either.
- **Overlapping blobs** — two clusters overlapping in the middle; points there are equidistant from both centroids.

We need a metric capturing **cohesion AND separation** at once.

## The Silhouette Score

Rousseeuw (1987) scores every point in $[-1, 1]$ by how well it fits its cluster versus the best alternative.

**Intra-cluster distance** $a(i)$ — mean distance from $x_i$ to the other members of its own cluster $C_k$ (cohesion; small = deep inside):

$$a(i) = \frac{1}{|C_k| - 1} \sum_{j \in C_k,\, j \neq i} d(x_i, x_j)$$

**Nearest-cluster distance** $b(i)$ — the smallest mean distance to any *other* cluster (large = well-justified assignment):

$$b(i) = \min_{k' \neq k} \frac{1}{|C_{k'}|} \sum_{j \in C_{k'}} d(x_i, x_j)$$

**Silhouette** of point $i$:

$$s(i) = \frac{b(i) - a(i)}{\max(a(i), b(i))}$$

<DerivationSteps>
<DerivationStep number={1} title="a < b — correctly clustered">
$\max = b$, so $s = 1 - a/b \in (0, 1]$ — near 1 when the cluster is tight and the competitor far.
</DerivationStep>
<DerivationStep number={2} title="a = b — boundary">
$s = 0$ — equidistant from own cluster and nearest competitor.
</DerivationStep>
<DerivationStep number={3} title="a > b — likely misclustered">
$\max = a$, so $s = b/a - 1 \in [-1, 0)$ — on average closer to a competing cluster.
</DerivationStep>
</DerivationSteps>

Overall score $S = \frac{1}{n}\sum_i s(i) \in [-1, 1]$:

| Score | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| 0.71–1.00 | Strong structure |
| 0.51–0.70 | Reasonable |
| 0.26–0.50 | Weak; maybe artificial |
| 0.00–0.25 | No substantial structure |
| < 0 | Points likely in wrong cluster |

**Worked example.** Cluster A: $x_1=(0,0), x_2=(1,0)$; Cluster B: $x_3=(5,0), x_4=(6,0)$. For $x_1$: $a = d(x_1,x_2) = 1$; $b = (5+6)/2 = 5.5$; $s = 4.5/5.5 = 0.818$.

| Point | a | b | s |
|---|---|---|---|
| $x_1$ | 1 | 5.5 | 0.818 |
| $x_2$ | 1 | 4.5 | 0.778 |
| $x_3$ | 1 | 4.5 | 0.778 |
| $x_4$ | 1 | 5.5 | 0.818 |

Overall $S = 0.798$ — two tight, well-separated clusters.

## Silhouette Plots

A horizontal bar per point, width $= s(i)$, grouped by cluster.

<ConceptBox title="Reading it">
- **Section thickness** = cluster size (balanced clustering → similar thickness).
- **Bar length** = confidence; uniformly long positive bars = confident assignment, short/uneven = fuzzy or overlapping.
- **Negative bars** = points closer to another cluster → reassignment candidates.
- **Average line** = mean silhouette; if many bars fall below it, K is probably wrong.
</ConceptBox>

## Other Metrics

**Davies-Bouldin** (1979) — average worst-case overlap, on cluster summaries (cheaper than silhouette). Scatter $s_k = \frac{1}{|C_k|}\sum_{i \in C_k}\|x_i - \mu_k\|$; similarity $R_{ij} = \frac{s_i + s_j}{d(\mu_i, \mu_j)}$ (large when clusters are fuzzy or close):

$$DB = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K} \max_{j \neq k} R_{kj}$$

**Lower is better**; not bounded above — compare only within a dataset.

**Calinski-Harabasz** (1974) — variance ratio (like ANOVA's F):

$$CH(K) = \frac{\text{BCSS}/(K-1)}{\text{WCSS}/(n-K)}, \qquad \text{BCSS} = \sum_k |C_k|\,\|\mu_k - \mu\|^2$$

**Higher is better**; can rise monotonically with K, so use comparatively.

## Choosing K

No single metric is definitive — use several and look for agreement:

| Method | Measures | Optimum | Non-spherical? |
|---|---|---|---|
| WCSS (elbow) | Cohesion | Kink | No |
| Gap statistic | Cohesion vs random | Max gap | Better |
| Silhouette | Cohesion + separation | Max mean s | Yes |
| Davies-Bouldin | Worst-case overlap | Min DB | Partly |
| Calinski-Harabasz | BCSS/WCSS | Max CH | Partly |

**Workflow:** run K = 2–10 with restarts (n_init ≥ 10); note the elbow; compute mean silhouette per K (note the max); compare DB and CH; pick where 2–3 metrics agree; inspect silhouette plots for the top candidates.
