## Learn the upsampling, don't hand-pick it

<PredictReveal
  prompt="A generator needs to go from a small spatial tensor to a full-resolution image — the opposite of what pooling does. Why does DCGAN avoid pooling layers entirely, in both G and D, rather than just using unpooling to reverse it?"
  options={["Pooling and unpooling are fixed, non-learned operations — replacing them with strided convolutions lets the network learn its own spatial resizing filters", "Pooling layers are simply too slow to run on a GPU"]}
  correct={0}
>

<DCGANArchitectureWidget />

Max pooling and its reverse are fixed rules — "take the max" or "spread the value back out" — with no learnable parameters. DCGAN replaces every pooling layer with a **strided convolution** in D (learned downsampling) and a **fractionally-strided (transposed) convolution** in G (learned upsampling, the same operation covered in the Transfer Learning track's U-Net modules). The network decides how to resize, rather than being told a fixed rule, which gives it far more capacity to shape what detail survives at each resolution.

</PredictReveal>

## The rest of the recipe

<PredictReveal
  prompt="DCGAN applies BatchNorm to almost every layer of both G and D — except two specific layers: G's output layer and D's input layer. What's the shared reason for both exceptions?"
  options={["BatchNorm is too slow at those two specific layers", "Those are the layers touching the actual image pixels, and normalizing them would distort the real data distribution or the desired output range"]}
  correct={1}
>

BatchNorm stabilizes training by keeping activations well-scaled through the network, but at the two layers that touch real image values directly, that normalization would work against the goal: D's input layer should see the true pixel statistics of real vs. fake images unmodified, and G's output layer needs to land in a fixed range (typically $[-1, 1]$ via Tanh) matching the normalized real images, not a batch-dependent one.

<ConceptBox title="TL;DR">
- DCGAN is a specific set of architectural guidelines, not a new training objective — it made convolutional GANs on images train reliably.
- Pooling is replaced everywhere: **strided convolutions** for learned downsampling in D, **transposed convolutions** for learned upsampling in G.
- BatchNorm is used almost everywhere except the two layers touching real pixel values (G's output, D's input); fully-connected layers are removed except at the very first/last stage; ReLU drives G, LeakyReLU drives D.
</ConceptBox>
