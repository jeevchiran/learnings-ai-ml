## Diffusing in a smaller space

<PredictReveal
  prompt="Running T sequential U-Net passes on a 1024x1024 pixel image is expensive — every single step processes the full-resolution tensor. What does latent diffusion change to reduce this cost?"
  options={["It reduces T, running fewer denoising steps on the same pixel-resolution images", "It runs the entire forward/reverse process in a pretrained autoencoder's compressed latent space instead of pixel space, then decodes back to pixels only once at the end"]}
  correct={1}
>

A pretrained autoencoder (VAE track) first compresses a $1024\times1024$ image into a much smaller latent tensor — commonly downsampled by a factor of 8 per side, so roughly $64\times$ fewer values overall. Every diffusion step from Modules 2–8 — forward noising, the U-Net denoiser, reverse sampling — runs entirely on that compact latent, not on pixels. Only at the very end does a single decoder pass turn the final denoised latent back into a full-resolution image.

## Why this doesn't just trade one cost for another

<PredictReveal
  prompt="Doesn't compressing to a latent space just move the computational cost into the encoder/decoder instead of removing it?"
  options={["Yes — the total cost stays roughly the same either way", "No — the encoder and decoder each run exactly once per image, while the expensive part (the T sequential denoising steps) is what shrinks, since every one of those T steps now operates on a much smaller tensor"]}
  correct={1}
>

The encoder and decoder are each a **single** pass, run once per image, regardless of $T$. The part that gets multiplied by $T$ — the sequential denoising steps — is exactly the part now operating on a far smaller tensor. Since sampling cost scales with (tensor size) × (number of steps), shrinking the tensor size shrinks the dominant cost directly, while the one-time encode/decode overhead stays fixed and small by comparison. This is the practical reason Stable Diffusion and similar systems can run on consumer hardware at all.

<ConceptBox title="TL;DR">
- **Latent diffusion** runs the entire forward/reverse process from this track inside a pretrained autoencoder's compact latent space, not on pixels directly.
- A frozen encoder compresses the image once before diffusion starts; a frozen decoder reconstructs pixels once after it finishes — everything in between (Modules 2–8) is unchanged, just operating on smaller tensors.
- Because sampling cost scales with tensor size × number of steps, and only the encode/decode are one-time costs, this shrinks the dominant, $T$-multiplied cost directly — the practical reason models like Stable Diffusion are tractable at consumer scale.
</ConceptBox>
