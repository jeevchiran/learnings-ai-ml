## A Markov chain of tiny Gaussian steps

The forward process adds a small amount of Gaussian noise at each of $T$ steps:

$$q(x_t \mid x_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}\big(x_t;\ \sqrt{1-\beta_t}\, x_{t-1},\ \beta_t I\big)$$

$\beta_t$ is a small, fixed variance schedule (Module 7 covers how it's chosen). Each step only depends on the previous one — a Markov chain — and after enough steps, $x_T$ is indistinguishable from pure Gaussian noise, regardless of what $x_0$ started as.

Because each step just adds independent Gaussian noise, the reparameterization trick (from the VAE track) applies here too: the composition of $t$ Gaussian steps is itself Gaussian, with a closed form

$$q(x_t \mid x_0) = \mathcal{N}\big(x_t;\ \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\, x_0,\ (1-\bar\alpha_t) I\big), \qquad \bar\alpha_t = \prod_{s=1}^{t} (1-\beta_s)$$

which means $x_t$ can be sampled in a **single step**: $x_t = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\, x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\, \epsilon$, for $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$. Training never needs to simulate the chain — it just picks a random $t$, draws one $\epsilon$, and computes $x_t$ directly.

</PredictReveal>

## What ᾱ_t means

<PredictReveal
  prompt="ᾱ_t is the product of (1 - β_s) for every step up to t. As t increases toward T, what happens to ᾱ_t, and what does that mean for how much of x_0 remains visible in x_t?"
  options={["ᾱ_t increases toward 1, so more and more of the original signal remains", "ᾱ_t decreases toward 0, so x_t becomes almost entirely noise with very little signal left"]}
  correct={1}
>

Each factor $(1-\beta_s)$ is slightly less than 1, so the product $\bar\alpha_t$ shrinks toward 0 as $t$ grows. Since $x_t = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\, x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\, \epsilon$, a small $\bar\alpha_t$ means the $x_0$ term is scaled down to almost nothing while the noise term's coefficient approaches 1 — exactly the "$x_T$ is indistinguishable from pure noise" property the chain is designed to have.

</PredictReveal>

]} />

<ConceptBox title="TL;DR">
- The forward process is a fixed Markov chain adding small Gaussian noise at each step: $q(x_t|x_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}(\sqrt{1-\beta_t}\,x_{t-1}, \beta_t I)$.
- Composing $t$ Gaussian steps is itself Gaussian, giving a **closed form** $x_t = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon$ — any $x_t$ can be sampled directly, with no need to simulate the chain step by step.
- $\bar\alpha_t = \prod_{s\le t}(1-\beta_s)$ shrinks toward 0 as $t \to T$, which is exactly why $x_T$ ends up indistinguishable from pure noise regardless of $x_0$.
</ConceptBox>
