# Module 8: Classifier-Free Guidance

<Bridge>

</Bridge>

## One network, two behaviors

<PredictReveal
  prompt="To make a diffusion model generate specifically 'a photo of a cat' rather than a random image, the simplest approach conditions ε_θ on a text/label embedding y: ε_θ(x_t, t, y). Is that alone enough to get strong prompt-following behavior in practice?"
  options={["Yes — conditioning the network on y is sufficient on its own", "Not strongly enough — the model needs to be pushed further toward y than a single conditional prediction naturally goes"]}
  correct={1}
>

Simply conditioning on $y$ produces a model that pays *some* attention to the prompt, but not as strongly as most applications want. **Classifier-free guidance** trains a single network to produce both a conditional prediction $\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, y)$ and an unconditional one $\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, \varnothing)$ — by randomly dropping the conditioning during training some fraction of the time — and then, at sampling time, extrapolates *away* from the unconditional prediction and *toward* the conditional one:

$$\hat\epsilon = \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, \varnothing) + w \cdot \big(\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, y) - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, \varnothing)\big)$$

</PredictReveal>

## What the guidance scale w actually does

<PredictReveal
  prompt="w=1 recovers the plain conditional prediction with no extrapolation. As w increases past 1, what happens to the trade-off between how closely samples follow the prompt and how varied they are?"
  options={["Both prompt-adherence and diversity increase together with no trade-off", "Prompt-adherence increases, but diversity decreases — pushed too far, samples over-saturate and start looking alike"]}
  correct={1}
>

<GuidanceScaleWidget />

$w$ is a single dial controlling how far to extrapolate past the conditional prediction. Larger $w$ pushes samples to match the prompt more closely — but that same extrapolation narrows the range of outputs the model is willing to produce, since it's amplifying the *direction* the label points in rather than sampling more broadly around it. This is a direct, deliberate trade-off, not a bug: most production systems pick a fixed $w$ (commonly somewhere in the single digits) that balances the two, informed by exactly this trade-off curve.

</PredictReveal>

<Recall items={[
  { q: "Why does increasing the guidance scale w improve prompt adherence at the cost of sample diversity?", a: "Larger w extrapolates further in the direction from the unconditional prediction toward the conditional one, amplifying alignment with the prompt. That same amplification narrows the range of outputs the model produces, since it's pushing samples further along one specific direction rather than sampling broadly — trading diversity for adherence." },
]} />

<ConceptBox title="TL;DR">
- **Classifier-free guidance** trains one network to produce both a conditional prediction $\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t,y)$ and an unconditional one $\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t,\varnothing)$, by randomly dropping conditioning during training.
- At sampling time, the guidance scale $w$ **extrapolates** past the conditional prediction, away from the unconditional one — pushing results further toward the prompt than plain conditioning alone would.
- Increasing $w$ trades **diversity for prompt adherence**: too high, and samples over-saturate and lose variety; the right value balances the two.
</ConceptBox>
